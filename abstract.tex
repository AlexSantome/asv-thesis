The continuous demand for higher performance, energy efficiency, and scalability in information 
processing systems has driven integrated technologies beyond the limits of purely electronic 
solutions. Although electronic integrated circuits have enabled major advances in computing and 
communication, fundamental constraints related to bandwidth, power consumption, and interconnect 
scalability have become increasingly significant. In this context, programmable integrated 
photonics has emerged as a promising paradigm, offering software-controlled reconfiguration on a 
common hardware platform. However, transitioning these versatile processors from foundational 
laboratory prototypes to mass-manufacturable systems remains constrained by the "scalability 
wall"—a set of critical bottlenecks encompassing cumulative optical losses, physical footprint 
constraints, and ultra-dense electrical control.

The primary objective of this doctoral thesis is to overcome these limitations and establish a 
reliable technological foundation for large-scale programmable photonic integrated circuits. At the 
device level, this work leverages advanced adjoint shape optimization to design highly 
fault-tolerant silicon-on-insulator building blocks. Notably, the optimized multi-mode 
interferometers achieve near-ideal 50/50 power splitting with peak excess losses as low as 0.077 dB 
across the extended C-band. Coupled with highly tolerant inverted tapers for edge coupling, these 
advancements establish a robust physical backbone for ultra-dense integration.

Scaling upwards to the circuit and architectural dimensions, this research overcomes traditional 
wire-bonding bottlenecks by validating area-array flip-chip packaging. This paradigm shift enabled 
the successful integration and control of a massive-scale smart transceiver comprising 1020 
programmable unit cells and hundreds of distributed photodetectors. Furthermore, the thesis 
pioneers a hybrid processing topology that seamlessly embeds specialized high-performance building 
blocks—such as arrayed waveguide gratings and multi-stage lattice filters—within a general-purpose 
hexagonal mesh. To resolve the computational challenges of simulating such massive architectures, 
a novel inductive mathematical modeling framework based on recursive signal flow graphs is 
introduced.

Finally, the practical viability of the scaled architecture is experimentally validated across 
rigorous real-world network applications. Empirical characterization of the integrated 
polarization diversity subsystem demonstrates the robust handling of high-speed 400G data streams, 
identifying strict sensitivity limits to differential group delay. Broadening the architectural 
scope to address explosive datacenter traffic, the thesis successfully demonstrates 
point-to-multipoint optical routing using a software-defined 32x32 optical circuit switch, 
achieving error-free 400G LAN-WDM multicasting up to a 1x4 radix.

Overall, this research validates the transformative capability of software-defined photonics, 
advancing the technology toward robust, scalable platforms capable of addressing the dynamic 
bandwidth and low-latency demands of next-generation telecommunication infrastructures and 
AI-driven data centers.
