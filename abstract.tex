The continuous demand for higher performance, energy efficiency, and scalability in information 
processing systems has driven integrated technologies beyond the limits of purely electronic 
architectures. Although electronic integrated circuits have enabled decades of technological 
advancement, fundamental physical constraints related to bandwidth, power dissipation, and 
interconnect density have become increasingly critical bottlenecks. Programmable integrated 
photonics has emerged as a compelling paradigm to overcome these limitations, offering 
software-controlled reconfigurability on a common hardware platform. However, transitioning these 
versatile processors from foundational laboratory prototypes to mass-manufacturable systems is 
tightly constrained by a "scalability wall"—a multifaceted bottleneck encompassing cumulative 
optical losses, physical footprint limitations, and ultra-dense electrical control interfaces.

To systematically overcome this scalability wall, this doctoral thesis establishes a reliable 
technological foundation for large-scale programmable photonic integrated circuits by addressing 
key limitations across four hierarchical dimensions: device, circuit, architecture, and 
system-level application. At the foundational device level, adjoint shape optimization is leveraged 
to design highly fault-tolerant silicon-on-insulator (SOI) building blocks capable of minimizing 
cumulative routing penalties. Notably, the optimized multi-mode interferometers achieved a 
near-ideal 50/50 power splitting ratio with peak excess losses as low as 0.077 dB across the 
extended C-band. Integrated alongside robust inverted tapers designed for edge coupling, these 
building blocks establish a very low-loss physical backbone capable of sustaining high-density 
topologies.

Capitalizing on this optimized physical layer, the architectural scaling from individual cells to 
large-scale circuits is realized by validating area-array flip-chip packaging as an alternative to 
traditional perimeter wire-bonding constraints. This packaging paradigm shift enabled the 
successful integration and simultaneous electrical control of a very high-density smart transceiver 
comprising 1020 programmable unit cells and 332 distributed photodetectors. To transcend the 
functional limits of general-purpose meshes, this research introduces a hybrid processing topology 
that integrates specialized, high-performance building blocks—specifically 35-channel arrayed 
waveguide gratings (AWGs) and multi-stage lattice filters—within a generic hexagonal core. 
Furthermore, to resolve the complex challenges of simulating these recirculating fabrics, a novel 
inductive mathematical modeling framework based on recursive signal flow graphs is formulated and 
verified.

Finally, the practical viability of this architecture is experimentally validated under rigorous 
operating conditions in realistic network scenarios. Dynamic characterization of the integrated 
polarization diversity subsystem demonstrates the robust handling of high-speed 400G data streams, 
revealing strict receiver DSP sensitivity thresholds to differential group delay (DGD). Broadening 
the evaluation scope to address the massive traffic growth in data centers, the thesis successfully 
demonstrates point-to-multipoint optical routing using a software-defined 32x32 optical circuit 
switch. By doing so, error-free 400G LAN-WDM multicasting up to a 1x4 radix is achieved. Overall, 
the contributions of this research validate the transformative capability of software-defined 
photonics, advancing the technology toward robust, manufacturable platforms capable of meeting the 
dynamic bandwidth and low-latency demands of next-generation telecommunication infrastructures and 
AI-driven data centers.
